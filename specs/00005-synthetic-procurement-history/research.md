# Research: Synthetic Procurement History

> Feature: E005 Synthetic Procurement History | Date: 2026-07-26 | Purpose: ground the censoring share, the duration family, the hierarchical signal strength, the datasheet contract, and the reproducibility claim for a seeded 200-line procurement lifecycle generator.

## Right-censoring share

- **Decision**: Do not pick a censoring percentage. Fix an explicit as-of (snapshot) date, let administrative censoring fall out of the order-arrival process against the duration draw, and record the *realized* share plus the resulting uncensored-event count as generator outputs.
- **Rationale**: Simulation literature treats censoring rate as a swept design factor (0–50% grids; 10/20/30/40% typical), not a canonical constant — so there is no defensible "correct" number to cite. What is defensible is the mechanism: administrative censoring at a data cutoff is the standard construction, and it is exactly what a procurement snapshot physically is. Precision is then driven by event count, not line count.
- **Pitfalls**: Too low and the dataset cannot *fail* a model that ignores censoring, so censoring-aware estimation is asserted rather than exercised. Too high and the tail becomes unestimable — Greenwood and Peto variance estimators substantially understate true variance in the tails under moderate-to-heavy censoring, and a median is undefined once fewer than half the lines have delivered.
- **Implication for the spec**: Require the generator to take an as-of date rather than a censoring rate, and require it to emit and assert the realized censored share and uncensored-event count, with a stated minimum event count rather than a stated censoring percentage.
- **Sources**: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11395225/>, <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2022.793648/full>

## Right-skewed duration generation

- **Decision**: Draw per-transition durations lognormally on the log scale, and disclose the family, its parameterization, units, rounding rule, and any truncation in the datasheet.
- **Rationale**: Gamma, Weibull, and lognormal are near-indistinguishable at matched mean and variance, so the discriminator is hazard shape, not fit. Weibull and gamma force a monotone hazard; lognormal and log-logistic admit a non-monotone (rise-then-fall) hazard, which is what a review or fabrication queue actually looks like — risk of imminent completion rises, then decays for the stuck items. Lognormal also composes cleanly with an additive per-vendor offset on the log scale, which is how the hierarchical layer below is induced.
- **Pitfalls**: Parameterization is the reproducibility trap, not the family. NumPy's `Generator.weibull` takes *shape only* — the scale must be applied by the caller — and gamma is shape/scale while much of the literature is shape/rate. Lognormal in NumPy is the mean and sigma of the *underlying normal*, not of the observable. Rounding durations to whole days after the draw changes the realized distribution and must be disclosed as part of it.
- **Implication for the spec**: Require the datasheet to state, per transition, the distribution family, the exact parameter names and values in the generator's own parameterization, the time unit, the rounding/truncation rule, and any minimum-duration floor.
- **Sources**: <https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/eha/vignettes/parametric.html>, <https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/generated/numpy.random.Generator.weibull.html>

## Hierarchical structure and recorded ground truth

- **Decision**: Induce vendor variation as an additive offset on the log-duration scale with between-vendor SD τ set relative to within-vendor SD σ, deliberately *unbalance* the per-vendor line counts, and write the realized per-vendor offsets to a ground-truth artifact separate from the dataset.
- **Rationale**: The shrinkage factor is ρⱼ = τ²/(τ² + σ²/nⱼ). At 200 lines over 12 vendors (n̄ ≈ 17), τ ≈ 0.24σ gives ρ ≈ 0.5; τ below roughly 0.12σ collapses toward complete pooling and τ above roughly 0.49σ toward no pooling. Both extremes make partial pooling unfalsifiable. Equally important: with *equal* group sizes every vendor gets the same ρ, so a balanced design demonstrates nothing about pooling that a single global shrinkage constant would not — unequal nⱼ is what makes differential shrinkage visible. Recording the drawn offsets is the standard parameter-recovery / simulation-based-calibration setup: a later model is scored by whether its posterior intervals cover the known values.
- **Pitfalls**: Twelve groups is few. Gelman's guidance is that a uniform prior on the hierarchical SD is acceptable from about five groups and a half-t is preferable when groups are few, precisely because τ itself is poorly determined at that J. Scoring the downstream model on recovering τ is therefore not a fair criterion; scoring coverage of the per-vendor offsets is. The ground-truth artifact must also be unreadable by the fitting path, or it leaks.
- **Implication for the spec**: Require a separate machine-readable ground-truth artifact recording σ, τ, and every realized per-vendor offset; require per-vendor line counts to be deliberately unequal with the spread disclosed; and require the recorded τ/σ ratio to sit in a stated band where shrinkage is neither near-zero nor near-one.
- **Sources**: <https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/published/taumain.pdf>, <https://mc-stan.org/docs/stan-users-guide/simulation-based-calibration.html>

## Dataset datasheets

- **Decision**: Follow the Gebru seven-section structure — Motivation, Composition, Collection Process, Preprocessing/Cleaning/Labeling, Uses, Distribution, Maintenance — reading "Collection Process" as *Generation Process*, and add the synthetic-specific disclosures the collected-data form does not cover.
- **Rationale**: The seven sections are already this project's precedent (the E001 roster datasheet uses them and omits Maintenance with a stated reason). What a collected dataset never has to answer is what an auditable synthetic one must: generator identity and revision, the seed and seed-derivation scheme, every distributional assumption with its parameters, the fixture inputs and their content hashes, the realized-versus-intended shape, and an explicit statement of what the dataset does *not* evidence. Emerging synthetic-data guidance converges on the same list — generating entity, models used, source inputs, explicit statements of built-in assumptions, intended use, known limitations. **Revisited 2026-07-27**: this record was written when the roster fixture was the only generation input, and it understates the obligation by two — the input set is now the roster fixture, E002's equipment-category map and E002's manufacturer catalog, each disclosed with its own `digest_kind`. `data-model.md` is normative for the fixture's shape and already carries all three; this line is corrected so the research record does not understate what it recommended.
- **Pitfalls**: A datasheet stating intended parameters without realized statistics is unauditable — a reader cannot tell whether the generator did what it claimed. The project's own recorded limitation (200 lines insufficient for vendor-level tail behaviour) belongs in the datasheet's Uses section as a negative claim, not only in the plan.
- **Implication for the spec**: Require the datasheet to carry all seven sections, and require the Generation Process section to disclose generator identity and revision, seed and seed-derivation scheme, per-transition distributional parameters, roster fixture hash, realized shape statistics against intended, and an explicit not-evidenced-by-this-dataset statement.
- **Sources**: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010>, <https://www.techpolicy.press/the-urgency-of-standards-for-synthetic-data-in-the-era-of-agentic-ai/>

## Reproducibility of a seeded generator

- **Decision**: Derive one independent child stream per purchase-order line via `SeedSequence.spawn` (or `default_rng([line_index, root_seed])`) from a single recorded root seed, pin NumPy exactly, and verify reproducibility by comparing a content hash of the canonically-serialized dataset rather than by asserting cross-version stream identity.
- **Rationale**: NumPy's stated guarantee for `Generator` is same seed, same bit generator, same call sequence, *same NumPy build, same environment, same machine* — and NumPy explicitly reserves the right to break `Generator` stream compatibility on feature (X.Y) releases. "Seeded therefore reproducible" is false across a version bump unless the version is pinned and the claim is scoped to it. Spawning also removes the more common failure: with a single sequential stream, inserting or reordering one line shifts every subsequent draw, so the dataset is reproducible but not *stable* under edit.
- **Pitfalls**: Adding a worker index to the root seed (`root_seed + i`) produces overlapping streams and is called out as unsafe; prepend it instead. Beyond RNG: iteration over sets or over dicts keyed by hash-randomized strings, any parallelism in the write path, and locale- or timezone-dependent date formatting all break byte-exactness while leaving the seed apparently honoured.
- **Implication for the spec**: Require a recorded root seed plus per-line derived substreams, an exact NumPy pin, a committed content hash of the canonical serialization as the reproducibility oracle, and an assertion that output ordering is deterministic and independent of set/dict iteration and of any parallelism.
- **Sources**: <https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/compatibility.html>, <https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/parallel.html>

## Open questions for clarification

Places where the evidence does not support a specific number, and a fabricated constant would be worse than an open question.

- **Censoring share has no canonical value.** The literature sweeps it; it does not standardise it. The recorded assumption of ~120 uncensored events after held-out splitting implies a censored share somewhere between roughly 25% and 40% depending on whether 120 counts the training subset or the whole dataset, and on the split fraction. The spec must state which of the three quantities — as-of date, split fraction, censored share — is fixed and which two are derived.
- **The ±4 percentage-point calibration bound needs its convention stated.** At 120 events, a 95% Wald half-width is about 8.9pp at p = 0.5, 5.4pp at p = 0.9, and 3.9pp at p = 0.95. So ±4pp is consistent with a one-standard-error band near p = 0.5, or with a 95% interval only near p ≈ 0.95. The spec should record the coverage level and the reference proportion, or the figure is not checkable. **Reassigned to E014, 2026-07-27.** This recommendation was written for whichever epic states the ±4pp bound, and E005 does not state it — the bound belongs to the forecast calibration, which E014 owns. E005 therefore publishes **no coverage level and no reference proportion**, and `test_gate_discharged.py` asserts that no E005 artifact does. Recorded rather than deleted: a research recommendation that was handed off reads, if left unannotated, exactly like one that was ignored.
- **The τ/σ band is derived, not cited.** The 0.12σ–0.49σ range is arithmetic from the standard shrinkage identity at n̄ ≈ 17, not a published convention. Treat it as a proposal to confirm.
- **Rework-loop rate has no external anchor found.** No source was located giving a conventional resubmittal rate for construction submittals. Declare it as a disclosed generator assumption with a stated basis, not as an empirical figure.

## Canonical serialization of the committed fixture

- **Decision**: Take the SHA-256 over a **canonical re-serialization of the parsed content**, not over the committed bytes. *(Superseded in part by plan AD-002: this topic originally recommended two NDJSON streams for diff readability. The plan reconciled to a single canonical `procurement-history.json` payload, because `canonical_bytes` takes one payload and E001's roster and E002's manifests are both single canonical JSON — two streams would have left the oracle's input undefined and added a third convention. The canonical-re-serialization decision below is unaffected and is the part that mattered.)* Reuse the existing rule set rather than authoring a third copy: `sort_keys=True`, `separators=(",", ":")`, `ensure_ascii=False`, UTF-8, no indentation, no trailing newline. **Those five properties describe the canonical byte string the digest is taken over — the in-memory re-serialization of the parsed payload — and not the committed file**, which is pretty-printed and ends with exactly one `\n` (`data-model.md` §Conventions). "No trailing newline" here and "exactly one trailing `\n`" there are therefore both correct and describe different byte strings; only the first is the reproducibility oracle, which is the whole point of hashing parsed content rather than file bytes. Emit **no floats** — `quantity` as a fixed-scale decimal string, durations as whole-day integers, and datasheet/ground-truth spreads quantized through `Decimal` to a declared scale and emitted as strings. Dates as `YYYY-MM-DD`; timestamps as tz-aware UTC with an explicit `timespec="seconds"`.
- **Rationale**: NDJSON makes a one-line change a one-line diff, while hashing re-serialized content rather than file bytes makes the oracle depend only on content — the property that survives a Windows checkout, CI on Linux, and a stash round-trip.
- **Rejected**: Pretty-printed single JSON, because a one-record change re-indents a nested blob and the reviewer cannot see the delta; CSV, because it loses the null-versus-empty distinction that nullable `from_state`, `note`, and `closing_event_id` depend on and Python's `csv` writes `\r\n` by default; hashing raw committed bytes, which couples the oracle to git's end-of-line handling for no gain.
- **Pitfalls**: `uuid.uuid4()` reads `os.urandom` and is **unaffected by any seed**, so surrogate keys drawn that way change the digest on every run — derive them instead. `datetime.isoformat()` at the default `timespec="auto"` omits the fractional part when microsecond is zero, so field width varies per row. `round()` on a binary float is not a canonical decimal (`round(2.675, 2)` is `2.67`), so quantization must go through `Decimal`. `json.dumps` emits non-standard `NaN`/`Infinity` unless `allow_nan=False`. Python's `sort_keys` orders by code point while RFC 8785 orders by UTF-16 code unit — identical for ASCII keys, divergent above U+FFFF.
- **Implication for the plan**: reuse `roster.reader.canonical_bytes` / `content_hash`; derive `po_line_id` and `event_id` deterministically — plan AD-003 computes them at load rather than writing them into the fixture, since they are a pure function of a natural key the payload already carries; fix `quantity`'s decimal scale; add `data/procurement/**/*.json text eol=lf` to `.gitattributes`.
- **Sources**: <https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785>, <https://docs.python.org/3/library/datetime.html#datetime.datetime.isoformat>

## Idempotent load under the delivered ordering constraints

- **Decision**: Stage, compare, then refuse or insert, all in one `REPEATABLE READ` transaction with `SET LOCAL TimeZone = 'UTC'`. `COPY` both record sets into unlogged `TEMP` staging tables carrying the same column types and **no constraints**, so staging load order is irrelevant. Reconcile in SQL with `EXCEPT ALL` in both directions over a comparison projection that excludes `created_at` (a `DEFAULT now()` column that would differ on every load) and both `GENERATED ALWAYS ... STORED` columns. Empty both ways is a no-op; a staging-side difference under an existing natural key is a content divergence and refuses; a target-side difference is the superset case and refuses. Insert all `purchase_order_line` rows first, then `lifecycle_event` rows sorted ascending by `(po_line_id, sequence_no)`, then commit — where the deferred closing foreign key validates.
- **Rationale**: "equal or refuse" is a set-comparison problem rather than a conflict-resolution one, so the comparison must run server-side against a staged copy before any write, which also makes the superset direction free.
- **Rejected**: `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` as the mechanism, because it silently tolerates a divergent row under the same natural key — exactly the case that must refuse; `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`, which performs the forbidden merge; a per-row `SELECT` pre-check, which costs N round trips and leaves a time-of-check window a staged comparison inside one transaction closes; `COPY` straight into the real tables, which cannot carry `ON CONFLICT` and would run the chain foreign key against an unvalidated set.
- **Pitfalls**: `execute_batch` does not exist in psycopg 3 — it is a psycopg2 `extras` helper, and psycopg 3.1+ `executemany` already uses pipeline mode internally. `numeric` equality ignores trailing zeros, so `EXCEPT` can pass where the text digest fails unless the fixture fixes `quantity`'s scale. A `FULL OUTER JOIN ... ON a.col = b.col` mishandles the three nullable columns and would need `IS NOT DISTINCT FROM`; `EXCEPT` uses not-distinct semantics but that is load-bearing here and should be pinned by a null-row test rather than trusted. `timestamptz` renders in the session time zone, so a non-UTC session produces a spurious refusal. psycopg 3's `executemany` issues individual pipelined statements rather than one multi-row insert, which is why ascending `sequence_no` is genuinely load-bearing.
- **Implication for the plan**: the loader is a staged-comparison design, not an upsert; `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` survives only as a concurrency guard paired with a `rowcount` assertion.
- **Sources**: <https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-set-constraints.html>, <https://www.psycopg.org/psycopg3/docs/advanced/pipeline.html>

## Open questions carried into the plan

- **Whether the chain foreign key's ascending-order requirement is strictly binding is unverified.** Row-level `AFTER` triggers fire at end of statement and referential checks are implemented that way, which implies a single multi-row insert would satisfy it in any order — but no documentation sentence was found saying so unambiguously for foreign keys, and psycopg 3's `executemany` does not produce a single multi-row statement regardless. Sorting is free; do not build on the end-of-statement behaviour.
- **`EXCEPT`'s null-equality semantics were not confirmed verbatim from official documentation.** The behaviour is standard SQL and almost certainly holds, but three nullable columns depend on it, so the plan requires a test that proves it rather than a citation.
- **No JSON shape has a PostgreSQL-native loader.** `COPY ... FROM` reads text, CSV, or binary. The loader parses the committed payload in Python and feeds `COPY ... FROM STDIN` a generated stream; the alternative of staging through `jsonb` and unpacking server-side was considered and is not free either. This holds under plan AD-002's single-payload shape exactly as it did under the two-stream draft.
