# Feature Specification: Synthetic Procurement History

**Feature Branch**: `00005-synthetic-procurement-history`
**Created**: 2026-07-26
**Status**: Draft
**Spec Type**: product
**Spec Maturity**: clarified
**Epic ID**: E005
**Epic Sources**: {PRD:CAP-001}
**Product Document**: `specs/prd.md`

## Problem Statement *(mandatory)*

The product forecasts delivery risk for open material orders, but no procurement lifecycle history exists to forecast from, and matched specification → submittal → purchase-order histories for a real project cannot be obtained from public sources. Without a dataset the forecast model has nothing to fit, the identity-resolution epic has no lines to match, and the worklist has nothing to rank — three P1 capabilities are blocked. A merely plausible dataset would be worse than none: it would let the system report calibration numbers that no reader could check, which is the unattributable-figure failure the product exists to avoid. What is needed is a generated history whose every assumption is disclosed and whose regeneration can be verified, so that a technical evaluator can audit the data the claims rest on rather than take it on trust.

## Scope *(mandatory)*

### Included

- A seeded generator producing 190–210 purchase-order lines across the 5 projects and 12 vendors already frozen in the E001 roster fixture.
- A lifecycle event history per line, conforming to the state machine E003 already enforces, including rework loops at a disclosed rate.
- Right-skewed durations per state transition, with per-vendor variation deliberately induced so that hierarchical partial pooling has real structure to recover.
- A need-by date and a criticality band (1–5, 5 = most critical) per line, criticality derived from material category and schedule pressure under a disclosed mapping.
- Right-censoring produced by an explicit as-of date rather than by targeting a censoring percentage: a share of lines are still in flight at the snapshot.
- Three committed artifacts: the **dataset fixture**, the **datasheet**, and a **ground-truth record** of the parameters the generator actually drew.
- A loader that applies the committed fixture into E003's `purchase_order_line` and `lifecycle_event` tables idempotently, and that refuses to proceed when the database and the fixture disagree.
- A validator that re-derives the dataset from the recorded seed and asserts it matches the committed artifact.

### Excluded

- **Any change to the procurement schema.** E003 owns `purchase_order_line` and `lifecycle_event` and they are delivered; this epic populates them and does not alter them. E005's reserved migration block `0200`–`0299` is claimed at epic start per governance and is expected to go unused.
- **Redeclaring projects or vendors.** The roster is E001's frozen fixture; this epic reads it and never restates its contents, so a roster change cannot silently diverge from the data generated against it.
- **Fitting or evaluating any model.** Recovering the generator's parameters is E007's work and scoring it is E014's; this epic only makes the truth recordable.
- **Document content for these lines.** Specifications and submittals are E002's corpus and E006's extraction; a purchase-order line here carries its own descriptive fields and no document reference.
- **Real vendor or firm names.** Excluded by the roster's own exclusion list, which this epic inherits rather than re-implements — for *projects and vendors*, whose identities come from the roster. Generated **manufacturer** names are a name space this epic invents rather than inherits, so the exclusion does not reach them by inheritance and FR-037 states it directly.

**Unresolved rather than excluded**: ownership of the held-out train/evaluation split is unallocated in the registered documents. FR-033 records that and assumes a fraction; Risk 3 tracks the consequence. Settling the ownership is an amendment on the default branch, not a decision this section records.

### Edge Cases & Boundaries

- A line still in its first state at the as-of date — a valid open line with exactly one lifecycle event and no terminal event.
- A line whose rework loop repeats a state: the loop repeats *states*, never sequence positions, because the event sequence is unique per line.
- A vendor drawing very few lines: per-vendor counts are deliberately unequal, so the smallest vendor must still produce a loadable, internally consistent history.
- An as-of date early enough that the uncensored event count falls below the required minimum — this must fail loudly rather than emit a thin dataset.
- Regeneration on a different machine or a different library version: the reproducibility claim is scoped to a pinned environment and must say so rather than overstate.
- Re-running the loader against a database already holding a *different* generation of the same natural keys — the row count would not change, so the loader must compare content rather than counts.
- Loading a regeneration produced at a new seed into a database holding the previous generation: the new purchase-order numbers collide on no natural key, so nothing is overwritten and both generations would come to rest side by side unless the load is refused outright.
- A roster edited after generation: the recorded roster hash no longer matches, and the mismatch must be detectable rather than silent.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory for product specs only)*

### User Story 1 - Generate the Procurement History (Priority: P1)

A developer runs a single command with a recorded seed and an as-of date. The generator reads the frozen roster, draws 190–210 purchase-order lines distributed unevenly across the 12 vendors, gives each a lifecycle history that obeys the delivered state machine, and writes three artifacts: the dataset fixture, the datasheet, and the ground-truth record. Nothing about the run depends on the machine it ran on beyond a pinned library version, and nothing about the roster is restated inside the generator.

**Why this priority**: Core value proposition — three P1 epics are blocked without this dataset, and nothing else in this epic has meaning until it exists.

**Independent Test**: Run the generator against the frozen roster with a fixed seed and confirm it emits all three artifacts, with line and event counts inside the intended shape.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the frozen E001 roster and a recorded root seed, **When** the generator runs, **Then** it writes a dataset fixture, a datasheet, and a ground-truth record, and every line's project and vendor identity comes from the roster.
2. **Given** a generated dataset, **When** per-vendor line counts are tallied, **Then** they are deliberately unequal and the spread is stated in the datasheet.
3. **Given** a generated dataset, **When** any line's lifecycle history is walked, **Then** every transition is one the delivered state machine permits and the first event is always `submitted`.
4. **Given** a generated dataset, **When** any line is inspected, **Then** its need-by date is not earlier than its order date, its criticality is an integer 1–5, and it carries the content hash of the roster it was generated against.
5. **Given** a roster edited since generation, **When** the generator or validator runs, **Then** the recorded roster hash no longer matches and the mismatch is reported rather than ignored.
6. **Given** an as-of date yielding fewer uncensored delivery events than the required minimum, **When** the generator runs, **Then** it fails and reports the shortfall rather than emitting the dataset.
7. **Given** an equipment-category map edited since generation, **When** the generator or validator runs, **Then** the recorded category-map digest no longer matches and the mismatch is reported **naming that input**, so the category-map half of FR-027 is exercised by its own scenario rather than inferred from the roster half.

### User Story 2 - Load the History into the Schema (Priority: P1)

A developer applies the committed fixture to the database. Lines and their event histories land in the tables E003 already delivered, satisfying every constraint those tables enforce. A second load changes nothing. A load is refused rather than silently leaving stale rows in place when the database holds a *different* generation — whether that shows up as differing content under the same natural keys, or as lines the fixture does not contain at all.

**Why this priority**: Without loading, the dataset is a file no downstream epic can query; E007 and E009 both read these tables, not this fixture.

**Independent Test**: Load the fixture into an empty migrated database, load it again, and confirm both the row counts and the row contents are unchanged.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an empty migrated database, **When** the fixture is loaded, **Then** every line and event is accepted by the delivered constraints with none disabled and none deferred beyond the one the schema already declares deferrable.
2. **Given** an already-loaded database, **When** the same fixture is loaded again, **Then** no line and no event is duplicated, and every row's content is unchanged.
3. **Given** a database holding a different generation under the same natural keys, **When** a load is attempted, **Then** it is refused and the divergence is reported.
4. **Given** a database holding lines the fixture does not contain, **When** a load is attempted, **Then** it is refused, so a regeneration at a new seed cannot come to rest alongside the generation already there.
5. **Given** a line that reached delivery, **When** it is loaded, **Then** it is marked closed and names its terminal event, and the closure invariant holds at commit.
6. **Given** a line still in flight at the as-of date, **When** it is loaded, **Then** it is open, names no closing event, and appears in the open-line index.

### User Story 3 - Reproduce the Dataset Exactly (Priority: P1)

A technical evaluator regenerates the dataset from the seed recorded in the datasheet and confirms it matches the committed artifact exactly. The check is a content comparison against a committed hash, not a claim about random-number streams, and the environment the claim holds in is stated rather than assumed.

**Why this priority**: The dataset's auditability is the whole reason it is synthetic rather than merely invented; an unreproducible generator makes every downstream number unverifiable.

**Independent Test**: Regenerate from the recorded seed in the pinned environment and confirm the canonical serialization hashes to the committed value.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the recorded root seed and the pinned environment, **When** the dataset is regenerated, **Then** its canonical serialization hashes to the value committed alongside it.
2. **Given** two runs at the same seed, **When** their outputs are compared, **Then** the ordering of lines and of events within a line is identical.
3. **Given** a dataset, **When** one line is added or the line order is changed and the run is repeated, **Then** no other line's generated values change.
4. **Given** a different root seed, **When** the generator runs, **Then** the output differs and the content hash does not match — the check can fail as well as pass.
5. **Given** a library version outside the pinned range, **When** reproduction is attempted, **Then** the scope limit is reported rather than a false reproduction claim being made.

### User Story 4 - Audit the Dataset from Its Datasheet (Priority: P1)

A technical evaluator reads the datasheet alone and can tell how every field came to exist: which distribution produced each duration and with which parameters, how criticality was assigned, what rate rework occurred at, what the as-of date was, what share of lines ended up censored, and what this dataset does *not* evidence. Intended shape and realized shape are both present, so a reader can see whether the generator did what it claimed.

**Why this priority**: A dataset whose assumptions are undisclosed cannot support a calibration claim; the epic's stated purpose is auditability, not plausibility.

**Independent Test**: Give the datasheet to a reader with no access to the generator source and confirm every generative assumption is recoverable from it.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the datasheet, **When** a reader looks for any per-transition duration assumption, **Then** the distribution family, the parameter names and values in the generator's own parameterization, the time unit, and the rounding rule are all stated.
2. **Given** the datasheet, **When** a reader looks for how criticality was assigned, **Then** the mapping from material category and schedule pressure to band is stated in full.
3. **Given** the datasheet, **When** a reader looks for the realized shape, **Then** the actual censored share, uncensored event count, per-vendor spread, between-vendor and within-vendor spread, and rework rate are recorded next to the intended values.
4. **Given** the datasheet, **When** a reader looks for limitations, **Then** each is recorded as scope decision, supporting evidence, reversal trigger, and production-scale alternative.
5. **Given** the datasheet, **When** a reader looks for provenance, **Then** the generator identity and revision, the root seed and how per-line seeds derive from it, the generation date, the SYNTHETIC layer label, and the roster fixture hash are all recorded.
6. **Given** the emitted artifacts, **When** they are examined for a train/evaluation split, **Then** none is present and the datasheet states that ownership of the split is unassigned.

### User Story 5 - Keep Known Truth Out of the Model's Reach (Priority: P2)

The ground-truth record exists so a later forecast model can be scored on whether it recovers the vendor effects the generator actually drew. That is only worth anything if the model cannot read the answers. This story owns the isolation: the record is reachable by a developer scoring a fit, and unreachable from the loaded tables and from any path a fitting job reads.

**Why this priority**: The record itself ships in User Story 1, and E007 and E014 do the scoring, so the MVP is complete without this. But an isolation property has its own failure mode — a truth file placed where a fitting job globs it leaks silently and invalidates every recovery claim made against it.

**Independent Test**: Confirm the record names every roster vendor with a realized offset, that no loaded table exposes those values, and that it sits outside every path a fitting job reads.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the ground-truth record, **When** it is read, **Then** it names the within-vendor spread, the between-vendor spread, and a realized offset for every vendor in the roster.
2. **Given** the loaded database, **When** it is queried for the generator's parameters, **Then** they are absent — the truth record is a separate artifact and no table carries it.
3. **Given** the ground-truth record and the dataset, **When** the two are compared, **Then** the offsets recorded are the ones the dataset was actually generated from.
4. **Given** the paths a model-fitting job reads, **When** they are enumerated, **Then** the ground-truth record is in none of them, and that is asserted by a build-gating check rather than by inspection.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements *(product specs only)*

- **FR-001**: System MUST read the project and vendor roster from the E001 fixture and MUST NOT restate any project or vendor identity in its own source or output configuration.
- **FR-002**: System MUST record the content hash of the roster fixture every line was generated against, carried once in the fixture envelope and stamped onto every row at load so the value reaches the storage boundary on each line, in the `sha256:<64 hex>` form the schema already enforces.
- **FR-003**: System MUST generate between 190 and 210 purchase-order lines inclusive, spanning all 5 projects and all 12 vendors in the roster, and MUST record the realized per-project split. Project allocation is a record-only obligation, not a bound: project is not a hierarchy level in the downstream forecast, so no statistical requirement attaches to it and none is stated — an unbounded MUST that nothing could breach would be worse than an honest record-only rule. Project is not a hierarchy level in the downstream forecast, so its allocation carries no statistical requirement beyond being stated.
- **FR-004**: System MUST allocate lines to vendors from a declared per-vendor count vector spanning roughly 5 to 35 lines and summing within FR-003's band, and MUST record that vector and its realized dispersion in the datasheet. The range is derived from FR-008's own shrinkage identity rather than chosen for appearance: at the stated spread ratio it puts implied shrinkage between roughly 0.22 and 0.67, a threefold span that makes pooling visibly *differential* across vendors. A narrow allocation would leave every vendor shrinking by nearly the same amount, which demonstrates nothing a single global constant would not. A declared vector rather than a draw, so the shape is auditable from the datasheet without re-running the generator and the smallest-vendor edge case has a known count to test at.
- **FR-005**: System MUST give every line a lifecycle event history whose transitions are all legal under the delivered state machine, beginning at `submitted`.
- **FR-006**: System MUST produce at least one rework loop in a **declared** number of lines targeting 30% of the realized line count, allocated across one, two and three loops in a declared integer split derived from 70/22/8 by largest-remainder apportionment, hard-capped at three, where a loop repeats a lifecycle state at a later sequence position rather than reusing a position. Two distinct senses of *declared* are in play here and the collision has already produced one cross-artifact contradiction, so they are separated explicitly: the **allocation** is declared rather than *drawn* (fixed integer counts, not a per-line random outcome), while the **rate** is declared rather than *cited* (chosen and disclosed, because no published convention exists). This sentence governs the first. The allocation is declared rather than drawn, following the precedent FR-004 already sets for per-vendor line counts, and for the same reason: a drawn share cannot be asserted without inventing a tolerance, and at this dataset's size no honest tolerance exists — a binomial at 30% over ~199 lines has a standard deviation near 3.3 percentage points, so a tight band fails on seed luck while a three-sigma band spans 20–40% and asserts nothing. Worse, the three-loop stratum is roughly five lines, and any tolerance wide enough to be fair admits zero, destroying the very property the split exists for. Declaring the counts makes the realized value equal the intended one exactly, so SC-004 asserts equality and needs no tolerance at all. The share and split are themselves declared, not cited — no published resubmittal convention was found — and are chosen for what they make observable: at this dataset's size they yield three non-degenerate strata for the downstream approval-cycle covariate, where a single-loop rule would make it binary. The cap is what turns the state machine's otherwise unbounded loop into a boundary a test can exercise at the maximum and one beyond it.
- **FR-007**: System MUST draw each state-transition duration from a right-skewed distribution whose per-transition location and scale are apportioned so the aggregate submitted-to-delivered duration targets a median of 61 days and an 80th percentile of 94 days, and MUST disclose the family, parameterization, time unit, rounding rule, any minimum-duration floor, and the per-transition apportionment. Those two figures are the product document's own published illustration of a delivery distribution, so the dataset is anchored to a number the project already stands behind rather than to an invented constant. A sum of independent right-skewed draws is not itself of that family, so the aggregate is a target the per-transition parameters approximately realize rather than an identity — that approximation is itself a disclosed assumption. The aggregate MUST be measured over the realized dataset **including FR-006's rework loops**, not over the six forward transitions alone: rework adds transitions and therefore duration, so parameters fitted before rework is applied land above both targets once it is. The **population** MUST be stated and MUST be *every* generated line's full submitted-to-delivered duration **as drawn, before the as-of date truncates it** — not the delivered subpopulation. A censored line has no observed submitted-to-delivered duration at all, and the delivered-only subpopulation is length-biased short by the as-of cut, so measuring there would compare the product document's population lead-time figures against a conditional distribution that is not them. The measurement is therefore a generation-time assertion over values the generator holds before truncation; the fixture, by design, does not carry a censored line's future. System MUST additionally record the delivered-only realized median and 80th percentile in the datasheet next to the population figures, so the size of the censoring bias is disclosed rather than merely asserted.
- **FR-008**: System MUST induce per-vendor duration variation targeting a between-vendor to within-vendor spread ratio of approximately 0.24, MUST hold the realized ratio between 0.12 and 0.49 inclusive, and MUST record both the realized ratio and the absolute between-vendor and within-vendor spreads it is computed from. FR-007's aggregate target implies a within-vendor log-scale spread of approximately 0.51, which puts the between-vendor spread at approximately 0.12 in absolute terms. Both figures come from the same derivation and neither is tightened beyond it: at roughly 17 lines per vendor the standard shrinkage identity puts the halfway point between complete and no pooling at 0.24, and puts the outer bounds — beyond which pooling collapses toward one extreme and becomes unfalsifiable — at 0.12 and 0.49.
- **FR-009**: System MUST take an explicit as-of date and an explicit order-date window as inputs, both committed as literal constants rather than defaulted, and MUST derive right-censoring from them rather than accepting a target censoring percentage. The as-of date MUST NOT default to the generation run date: a run-date default makes FR-021's committed content hash unreproducible the day after generation, failing SC-012 while the recorded seed still appears to have been honoured.
- **FR-010**: System MUST record the realized censored share and the realized uncensored delivery-event count as generator outputs, and MUST fail rather than emit a dataset carrying fewer uncensored delivery events than **the greater of 80% of realized lines and 160**, measured **in the full dataset, before any split**. Both bounds are needed: the share alone stops the censored ceiling moving across FR-003's band, but at 190 lines an 80% share yields 152 events and only 114 after a 0.25 split — below the product document's figure, with the criterion still green. A fixed count alone would cap the censored share at 15.8% at one end of FR-003's band and 23.8% at the other, so identical generative parameters could pass or fail on line count alone; the share removes that sensitivity and the absolute floor keeps the post-split claim true across the whole band. A count inside the resulting band is recorded, not rejected; both edges are actionable, since the censoring floor below rejects a delivered share that is too high just as the event floor rejects one that is too low. System MUST also fail when **fewer than 10%** of lines are censored, or when any non-terminal lifecycle state holds no line at the as-of date. Without a lower bound, a dataset in which every line has been delivered satisfies every other criterion in this spec while exercising none of the censoring this epic exists to produce, and the open-line index, the first-state edge case, and the in-flight load scenario would all pass vacuously. The product document's recorded figure of approximately 120 is a **post-split** count and this floor is its pre-split counterpart: at FR-033's assumed held-out fraction of 0.25, 120 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = 160. The two numbers are the same obligation measured at different points and must not be read as the same quantity. The three bounds MUST be stated and asserted as **one jointly admissible window**, not as thresholds each assertable alone: at a realized line count *N*, the delivered share MUST fall in `[max(0.80, 160/N), 0.90]`, which is non-empty across the whole of FR-003's band — `[84.2%, 90.0%]` at *N* = 190 and `[80.0%, 90.0%]` at *N* = 210. **The two floors change which one binds at exactly *N* = 200**: below 200 the absolute count of 160 is the binding floor, at and above 200 the 80% share is. Both regimes MUST be exercised, or whichever floor is slack across the sample actually run is asserted but never tested.
- **FR-011**: System MUST derive each line's need-by date as its order date plus the line's expected total duration plus a drawn slack, never earlier than the order date, and MUST calibrate the slack distribution so that between 25% and 35% of delivered lines miss their need-by date, recording the realized share. The 25-35% band is a chosen design target, and the departure is recorded rather than passed over: the product document's illustration that FR-007 draws its 61-day and 94-day figures from carries a third figure in the same sentence, a 38% chance of missing the need-by date. The band sits just below it. FR-007 takes the first two as dataset-level distributional targets; this requirement declines to take the third as a dataset-level rate, and a later pass may reasonably decide that is inconsistent and align at 38%. The late share is a disclosed design quantity rather than a by-product of the duration draw: a dataset in which nothing arrives late gives the downstream worklist nothing to rank and the evaluation harness no schedule harm to calibrate against.
- **FR-012**: System MUST define schedule pressure as a line's slack relative to its material category's expected duration, quantized into terciles over the realized dataset as a whole rather than within each category, with the cut points recorded, and MUST derive the criticality band from a disclosed material-category-tier by pressure-tercile table rather than drawing it independently of the rest of the line. Criticality is derived and slack is drawn, in that direction, so the derivation is not circular.
- **FR-013**: System MUST emit the dataset as a committed fixture whose serialization is canonical and byte-stable, under a canonicalization rule set that is normative rather than illustrative: object keys sorted, compact separators carrying no whitespace after `,` or `:`, non-ASCII characters emitted literally rather than escaped, UTF-8 encoding, no indentation, and no trailing newline. System MUST NOT emit a JSON float anywhere in the fixture — every real-valued field is a decimal string at a fixed scale and every other numeric field is an integer — because a binary float's repr is not a canonical decimal and the non-finite values have no standard JSON form. The canonical form is a **different byte string from the committed file**, which is pretty-printed and ends with exactly one newline; the rules above govern the canonical form alone, and FR-021 fixes which of the two is hashed.
- **FR-014**: System MUST emit a datasheet carrying all seven standard datasheet sections — Motivation, Composition, Generation Process, Preprocessing and Labeling, Uses, Distribution, and Maintenance — where Generation Process replaces the collection section a gathered dataset would carry.
- **FR-015**: System MUST disclose in the datasheet the generator identity and revision, the root seed, the scheme by which per-line seeds derive from it, the generation date, the `SYNTHETIC` layer label, the content hash of **every generation input** — the E001 roster fixture, E002's equipment-category map, and E002's manufacturer catalog, each named — and the realized shape statistics against the intended ones. System MUST also record, in the datasheet's Distribution section, that the fixture is not a corpus document and carries no corpus manifest entry, and that it is generated wholly by this project from a committed seed and the E001 roster under basis `project-generated-no-third-party-rights` with no third-party rights attaching.
- **FR-016**: System MUST record every limitation in the datasheet as four parts — the scope decision, its supporting evidence, the condition that would reverse it, and the production-scale alternative — and MUST record at minimum the dataset's insufficiency for validating vendor-level tail behaviour, the environment scope of the reproducibility claim, the declared-not-cited basis of the rework rate, the derived-not-cited basis of FR-008's spread-ratio target and band, the absence of manufacturer and part-number overlap with E002's corpus — so this dataset supports no cross-document identity resolution on the key E009 blocks with, reversible when E002 publishes those fields, with the production-scale alternative being a corpus that carries product data rather than transmittal fields alone — the duration model's status as a stand-in, the absence of row-level generation provenance — a loaded row carries the roster hash but not the dataset content hash or generator revision, so it is traceable to the roster it was generated against and not to the generation run that produced it — and the fact that the post-split uncensored event count is bounded only through FR-033's *assumed* held-out fraction of 0.25 — this epic neither performs the split nor observes its result, so a downstream epic splitting at a different fraction would leave the product document's recorded figure of approximately 120 unmet with nothing in this epic detecting it. The minimum set was **eight** subjects until 2026-07-26, when the manufacturer/part-number overlap subject was withdrawn: E002 published the fields, so the limitation it disclosed is no longer true and disclosing it would misinform the reader it exists to inform.
- **FR-017**: System MUST emit a machine-readable ground-truth record holding the within-vendor spread, the between-vendor spread, and every realized per-vendor offset.
- **FR-018**: System MUST keep the ground-truth record out of the loaded tables and outside every directory a model-fitting entry point resolves as an input root, so that no value in it can reach a model as an input. The check enforcing this MUST enumerate those roots from the fitting entry point's own configuration rather than from a hand-maintained exclusion list, because a list maintained by hand is precisely how a truth file comes to be read by a job that was written later. No path is fixed here; the boundary rule is what this requirement states and the paths belong to planning. The resolved root set MUST be **non-empty** and MUST contain the directory holding the dataset fixture, and an empty enumeration MUST fail the check rather than satisfy it. No model-fitting entry point exists yet, so an enumeration that resolves to nothing is the expected accident rather than a remote one: it would satisfy the isolation check over an empty set, and a vacuous pass is indistinguishable in a report from a satisfied one. The fixture's own directory is the one root known to be a fitting input today — a fitting job reads the dataset — which is what makes the non-emptiness obligation checkable before E007 lands rather than after.
- **FR-019**: System MUST produce a dataset in which adding or reordering one line changes no other line's generated values. Each line's random stream MUST therefore be derived from content that identifies the line, and **positional derivation is prohibited** — neither an index added to the root seed nor a single stream consumed line by line in emission order. Both are named because both fail the same way and neither is visible from the artifact: they shift every subsequent line's draws when one line is inserted or moved, and they leave the recorded seed apparently honoured while doing it.
- **FR-020**: System MUST produce identical output ordering across runs at the same seed in the pinned environment, emitting lines, and the events within a line, each in a stated total order over their own key. **No iteration over a set, over a mapping keyed by a hash-randomised string, or over a parallel work queue may reach the write path**, since each of the three reorders output between runs at the same seed while leaving the seed apparently honoured — the failure is in the write path rather than in the draw, so a stream-identity check would not catch it.
- **FR-021**: System MUST commit a content hash of the canonical serialization and MUST verify reproduction by comparing against that hash rather than by asserting random-number-stream identity. The hashed object is the canonical re-serialization of the **parsed** fixture payload, never the committed file's bytes: hashing the file would couple the oracle to indentation, to the trailing newline, and to git's end-of-line normalisation, none of which are content. Hashing the committed bytes in place of the parsed payload is prohibited.
- **FR-022**: System MUST scope its reproducibility claim to a pinned library version and MUST report the scope limit rather than claiming reproduction outside it. The pin recorded in the emitted artifacts MUST equal the version **actually resolved in the environment the run executed in**, so a recorded pin cannot name a version nothing ran under. The comparison that produces the scope-limit report MUST take the observed version as an **injected input** rather than reading it only from the running interpreter, so the out-of-pin direction is demonstrable without installing a second copy of the library — a report path that can only be reached by an environment CI cannot construct is an unevidenced claim.
- **FR-023**: System MUST load the committed fixture into the delivered `purchase_order_line` and `lifecycle_event` tables without altering either table's definition.
- **FR-024**: System MUST insert a line's lifecycle events in ascending sequence order, because the chain foreign key that links each event to its predecessor is not deferrable.
- **FR-025**: System MUST make a repeated load of the same fixture a no-op in both row count and row content, relying on the natural key the schema already declares for that purpose.
- **FR-026**: System MUST refuse a load when a row already present under a natural key differs in content from the fixture being loaded, and MUST report the divergence rather than leaving the stale row in place.
- **FR-027**: System MUST **refuse to proceed** on a mismatch between any recorded generation-input hash and the corresponding input present at validation time, exiting non-zero and naming the offending input together with both digests — not warning, and not recording the discrepancy and continuing. Each input MUST be recomputed under the digest convention it was recorded with, which the artifact carries per entry, so that a symmetric check cannot degrade into recomputing both under one convention and reporting a false mismatch on the other. The check covers **every** entry in `generation_inputs` — currently the E001 roster fixture, E002's equipment-category map and E002's manufacturer catalog — and MUST be driven by iterating that list rather than by naming the inputs, so an input added to the envelope cannot be added without also being checked. An edited category map changes what the dataset means exactly as an edited roster does, and an edited catalog changes which manufacturers the corpus overlap is built from, so checking a subset leaves the rest free to drift with every recorded hash still matching. ## Assumptions & Risks *(mandatory)*

### Assumptions

- The E001 roster is frozen for the life of this dataset; a roster change invalidates the recorded hash and requires regeneration rather than patching.

## Glossary *(include when spec introduces 2+ domain-specific terms)*

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Right-censoring | A line whose delivery has not happened by the as-of date, so its duration is known only to exceed the elapsed time rather than being observed. |
| As-of date | The snapshot date the dataset represents. Lines still in flight at this date are censored; it is an input, and the censored share is derived from it. |
| Rework loop | A return to an earlier lifecycle state — review sending a submittal back for resubmission. Repeats a state at a later sequence position, never a position. |
| Partial pooling | Estimating each vendor's behaviour using both its own lines and the population, so vendors with few lines borrow strength from the rest. |
| Between-vendor spread | How much vendors differ from one another in typical duration. Its ratio to within-vendor spread determines whether pooling is demonstrable. Distinct from the *per-vendor count dispersion* in FR-004, which is a spread of line counts, not of durations. |
| Schedule pressure | A line's slack relative to its material category's expected duration, quantized into terciles. One of the two inputs criticality is derived from. |
| Slack | The margin added beyond a line's expected total duration when setting its need-by date. Drawn, not derived; the need-by date follows from it. |
| Material category | The class of equipment a line orders, drawn from the committed equipment-category map E002 publishes rather than invented here. |
| Overlap share | Two shares, measured and reported separately. **FR-032's**: the proportion of lines satisfying the four-clause predicate on material category, description composition, quantity with unit, and vendor. **FR-034's**: the proportion whose `manufacturer` and `part_number` are drawn from E002's published catalog (clauses 5–6). Both are *vocabulary* overlap — shared vocabulary, not a document reference |
| Within-vendor spread | How much lines vary within a single vendor. |
| Ground-truth record | The machine-readable record of the parameters the generator actually drew, kept outside the dataset so a model cannot consume it. |
| Datasheet | The shipped disclosure document making the dataset auditable — every generative assumption, every realized statistic, and every limitation. |
| Canonical serialization | A byte-stable rendering of the dataset, so that content equality can be checked by hash rather than by parsing. |
| Criticality band | An ordinal 1–5 rating of how damaging a late delivery would be, 5 being most critical. |
